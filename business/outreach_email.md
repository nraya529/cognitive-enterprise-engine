# Cold Outreach Email Templates

Three variants tuned for the Boston SMB market — pick by industry. All are short on purpose. Long cold emails get deleted.

---

## Variant A — Trades (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, contractors)

**Subject:** Quick question about how you handle vendor invoices

Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

I'm {{YOUR_NAME}} — I help Boston-area trades businesses automate the administrative side of their operation so the office staff can stop re-typing invoices into QuickBooks all day.

Most {{TRADE}} businesses I talk to lose 15–20 hours a week to manual data entry between email, vendor invoices, and bookkeeping. If that's true for {{COMPANY_NAME}}, I'd be glad to walk you through what a fix looks like — no slide deck, just a 20-minute call where I show you the system running on your last invoice.

Worth a quick conversation?

— {{YOUR_NAME}}
{{YOUR_PHONE}} · {{YOUR_EMAIL}}

## Variant B — Local logistics / distribution

**Subject:** {{COMPANY_NAME}}'s invoice processing — 20-min idea

Hi {{FIRST_NAME}},

Distribution operations in the {{REGION}} area handle a brutal volume of vendor paperwork — bills of lading, supplier invoices, customer POs — most of it still keyed by hand into accounting software.

## Follow-up cadence

If no reply:

- **Day 4** — Short bump: *"{{FIRST_NAME}} — surfacing this in case it got buried. Happy to send a 90-second video showing the system in action instead of jumping straight to a call if that's easier."*
- **Day 10** — Value-add: *"Saw {{INDUSTRY_NEWS_REFERENCE}} this morning — relevant to ops at {{COMPANY_NAME}}. Still open to that call when timing is better."*
- **Day 21** — Break-up: *"Closing the loop on this — assuming the timing isn't right. I'll keep an eye on {{COMPANY_NAME}} and reach out again later in the year. Hope Q2 goes well."*

Do not chase past three messages. Move on.
