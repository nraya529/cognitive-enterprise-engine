# Discovery Call Script — 25 minutes

## Goal

Get enough information to send a custom proposal **the same day**. Not "send some thoughts." A real, numbered proposal.

## Opening — 2 minutes

> "Thanks for making the time, {{FIRST_NAME}}. I'm going to keep this tight — I want to understand how the back office actually works at {{COMPANY_NAME}} today, then I'll tell you whether what I do is a fit. If it isn't, I'll say so. Sound good?"

Wait for assent. Take notes in a single doc — they'll see typing, that's fine.

## Section 1: Operational baseline — 10 minutes

Five questions, in order. Do not skip.

1. **Walk me through what happens after a vendor sends you an invoice today.** From the moment it arrives to the moment it's paid.
   - Listen for: who touches it, how many systems, how long it takes, where it lives.
2. **How many of those do you process in a typical week? And what's the busiest week look like?**
   - Capture: average and peak volume.
3. **Who handles that work, and what would you rather they were doing instead?**
   - Critical question. The answer is the emotional lever for the close.
4. **When something goes wrong — wrong amount keyed, wrong account, missed payment — how do you usually find out?**
   - Listen for: month-end surprises, vendor angry calls, audit corrections.
5. **What accounting platform are you on, and how long have you been on it?**
   - Capture: QuickBooks Online / Desktop, Xero, Sage, FreshBooks, custom. Long tenure = high switching cost = automate around it, don't replace it.

## Section 2: Adjacent friction — 5 minutes

Quick pass on adjacent workflows so you can quote the right tier.

- "Do you collect W-9s from vendors? How does that work today?"
- "When you hire someone, what's the paperwork flow look like?"
- "Do you ever send the same email to a customer five times — confirmations, receipts, reminders?"

If they nod hard at any of these, you're upselling from Tier A to Tier B.

## Section 3: The cost frame — 3 minutes

Do the math live, on a shared screen or out loud.

> "Let me play this back. You said about {{N}} invoices a week, around {{M}} minutes each — that's roughly {{N*M/60}} hours a week. At, ballpark, $25 to $35 an hour fully loaded, you're spending around ${{WEEKLY_COST}} a week on this — call it ${{ANNUAL_COST}} a year just for the data-entry step. Does that pass your smell test?"

They will either correct you (good — refines the proposal) or say "yeah that sounds right" (excellent — you've anchored the value).

## Section 4: The close — 5 minutes

> "Okay — here's how I work. I build a custom system that takes that whole workflow and runs it autonomously. Two-week deployment, runs alongside your existing setup, doesn't replace anyone but frees them up. I have two tiers: a focused one at $5K plus a small monthly retainer, and a comprehensive one at $9.5K. Most clients your size go with the focused tier first and expand once they see it working."
>
> "I'd like to send you a numbered proposal today with specifics for {{COMPANY_NAME}}. If it makes sense, we sign by Friday and you're running by the end of the month. If it doesn't make sense, no hard feelings."
>
> "Fair?"

If they say yes — send the proposal within 4 hours of the call ending.

If they hesitate — ask: *"What's the question I haven't answered yet?"* and address it directly.

## Disqualifiers — walk away

Do not sign a client who:

- Cannot tell you what accounting platform they use
- Insists on switching their accounting platform as part of the project (out of scope, high failure rate)
- Processes < 20 documents per month (insufficient ROI; you'll churn)
- Has had > 2 failed consulting engagements in the past year (pattern — not them, you)

It is far cheaper to walk away from a bad-fit client than to deliver into one.

## Post-call

1. Send a one-paragraph thank-you within 30 minutes.
2. Send the customized proposal (use `proposal_template.md`) within 4 hours.
3. Set a calendar follow-up for 48 hours out.
